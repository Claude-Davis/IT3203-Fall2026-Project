The History of the Domain Naming System

The predecessor of the Domain Naming System (DNS) was the Internet Name Server, released in 1978. Later In 1983, employee of ISI, Paul Mockapetris, designed an early version of today’s DNS. At the time, this Internet component was called “Jeeves.” The need for the DNS started with rising the costs of adding machines to the growing network. The DNS changed the previous process of repeatedly transmitting requests by moving the client to another server if their request went unanswered. Over the next few years, the DNS had several implementations such as with the University of Southern California, SRI International’s Network Information Center, and other places. Its most known implementation was the Berkeley Internet Name Domain, BIND. When BIND was later included in the free version of the UNIX operating system, the DNS was able to spread widely through academia, business, etc. By 1986, new operating systems and machines were designed to rely solely on DNS.
