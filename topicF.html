Milestones

The Domain Naming System has experienced many notable milestones. Some of which are listed below:

  • 1978 - Introduction of the INS
  • 1983 - Introduction of the DNS
  • 1984 - Introduction of first TLDs
  • 1985 - Creation of Nordu.net
  • 1987 - DNS adopted as Internet standard
  • 1988 - IANA established
  • 1989 - The World offers direct Internet access
  • 1991 - The WWW is launched
  • 1993 - NSF becomes first domain name registrar

Introduction of the First Domain Name

            

                The first active domain name, launched in 1985, was nordu.net
                The initial purpose of this domain was to connect national research and educational computer networks between Nordic countries.
            

        


        

            
DNS as the Internet Standard

            

                The domain naming system was quick to gain users and had several implementations shortly after its launch.

                Its fast rise in popularity soon led to its adoption as the Internet standard in 1987, just four years after its creation.
            

        


        

            
Launch of the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority

            

                The Internet Assigned Numbers Authority, abbreviated IANA, was formally established in December 1988 by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency, DARPA.

                IANA's purpose is to to oversee the general administration of DNS and other Internet standards to maintain global Internet stability.

                For DNS, IANA oversees standard's root management, TLD coordination, and IP address allocation.
            

        


        

            
The Start of Direct Internet Access

            

                In 1989, the first U.S. commercial Internet service provider, or ISP, begins to offer direct access to the Internet.

                This ISP, called The World, allowed customers to access the Internet via dial-up connection.

                The World was notable as one of the first ISPs to offer a full connection to the Internet.
                This was a significant change from the previously used time-sharing tools and propriety online services.
                Time sharing systems would give users access to a single computer with no wider connection, and propriety online services were closed platforms whose link to the Internet was generally limited to emails.
            

        


        

            
Launch of the World Wide Web

            

                The World Wide Web (WWW) was headed by Tim Berners-Lee, an English computer scientist //. He launched The WWW in August 1991.

                The first ever website to be uploaded onto the WWW was info.cern.ch.
            

        


        

            
The First Domain Name Registrar

            

                In 1993, the National Science Foundation (NSF) enters into a contract with Network Solutions and is enabled to be the first domain name registrar.

                The NSF was given exclusive authority to manage the registrations for .com, .nrt, and .org.
